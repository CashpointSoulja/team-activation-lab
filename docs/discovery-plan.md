# Discovery and interview plan

**Status: not started. No interviews have been conducted and none are reported anywhere in this project.**

## Goal
Find out whether buyer-to-team activation is a real source of loss for B2B evaluations, and if so, where.

## Who (target 12–16 conversations, recruited with consent through CS)
| Segment | n | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Admins whose team reached two completions in week one | 4–5 | What worked |
| Admins who signed up but had ≤1 active learner after 14 days | 4–5 | Where it stalled |
| Sales / CS who ran recent evaluations | 3–4 | Patterns across many accounts |
| Learners invited in a trial | 2 | Invite and first-session friction |

## Questions (open, past-behaviour, no leading)
1. Walk me through the last time you evaluated training for your team. What did you need to see before buying?
2. After signing up, what was the first thing you tried to get the team to do?
3. Where did the evaluation slow down: picking content, getting people to join, time, or procurement?
4. How did you judge whether the trial was worth it? Who did you show it to?
5. Who else (Sales, CS, finance, security leadership) was involved, and when?

## Data review alongside interviews
- Event completeness for invite, accept and completion events (are we even seeing the steps?)
- Time-to-first-completion distribution, to check the 7-day window
- Whether historic "2 learners in 7 days" accounts retained or converted at higher rates (correlation only; it informs whether the metric is worth testing)

## Decision rules
- **Proceed to experiment** if ≥ half of stalled admins describe not knowing what to do first, or the data shows most loss between signup and first invite.
- **Change direction** (first falsifier) if stalled admins already had a clear plan and the blocker was procurement, budget, seats or security approval.
- Write up as an evidence log with quotes attributed to segment only.
